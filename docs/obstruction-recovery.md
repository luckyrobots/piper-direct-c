# Obstruction detection and recovery

Joint MoveJ, range-entry and synchronized/cycle motion monitor each joint independently. When a joint has more than the selected tolerance left to travel and its command leads the measurement by at least `max(0.05°, selected tolerance)`, the backend requests STOP after 0.5 seconds without at least 0.01° of progress toward its target. Tracking inside the accepted tolerance does not count as an obstruction. This avoids false alarms on short-travel joints during slow synchronized moves (the observed all-zero event had only 0.111° J6 tracking error at 0.5° tolerance). Other joints cannot conceal a stalled joint. Monitoring resets at each action and cycle leg. Existing driver-fault, stale-feedback, tracking-error and heartbeat checks remain. The separate standard CAN gripper retains its existing progress checks; Pika has its own serial controller.

This is a position-feedback stall heuristic, not a contact sensor. It cannot guarantee a stop within 0.5 seconds of physical contact, detect every creeping/slipping obstruction, or guarantee the delivery or braking time of a software STOP. Do not validate it by putting a hand in the robot's path. Use injected frozen feedback, then an appropriate controlled hardware fixture if physical validation is needed.

The first stop records wall-clock time, reason, joint number where detected, request ID, selected speed, measured/commanded/final angles, current when fresh, driver fault bits, controller status and per-joint progress age. Later STOP commands cannot overwrite that event until recovery clears the active incident. The event survives process restart. Old stops without evidence are identified as such.

The native panel's primary action is **Resting & clear — recover to zero**. Its single click confirms that the arm is securely resting and its route to zero is clear. It does not ask the user to lift the shoulder into range. A healthy enabled arm keeps its power; a stopped or partially enabled arm follows reset, fresh six-motor-off verification, power-only enable at its measured resting pose, bounded inward range correction, then zero at 10%. Range correction uses 1% speed and rejects corrections larger than 3°. The final state is verified within 1° of zero, with all motors enabled. Pika is unchanged and the previous movement is discarded. No previously interrupted trajectory restarts.

The secondary controller-only recovery remains available separately. If its reset times out with all six motors verified off and stationary, **Rest arm securely — retry failed reset** retries that reset explicitly; it cannot resend an active reset. Some firmware retains a teaching flag after STOP; that flag can be handled in this stopped, all-motors-off state, while genuine hand guiding and playback still block reset. Its pending recovery record clears only after fresh reset completion is verified. This secondary reset/verification does not enable motors or move to zero.

Motor enable remains a power-only operation. It sends explicit driver indices 1–6 at a paced interval, so a delayed acknowledgment from one driver does not starve the others. Each joint's holding baseline freezes immediately before its first enable request and is never reset by retries. The 0.5° drift guard applies from that first request; joints not yet requested allow up to 1° total supported movement. Every driver must provide feedback newer than its own request, and all six must settle for 0.4 seconds before the UI reports completion. Loss of an acknowledged enable stops startup. This avoids treating a still-unpowered wrist as if it were already holding a position.

The web dashboard displays the event and live controller state, reset/verify steps, teaching-state restrictions and range issues. The native Pipers panel chooses the required stages from telemetry and verifies each stage automatically. Reset can remove holding torque; the arm stays on its secure support during that stage. Unknown/driver faults, missing feedback and active teaching withhold recovery. Cameras remain available for inspection. Measured scene tracking continues through recovery; simulation following is discarded and requires an explicit new session. The dashboard never rewrites calibration to resolve an obstruction.

For an interrupted live-follow stream, a verified measured hold preserves enabled motors instead of using controller STOP for an ordinary scheduler or source-stream interruption. If the hold cannot be verified, or a genuine hardware fault requires STOP, the original failure is retained in the incident diagnostics.

## Offline validation

- Detector tests: frozen joint threshold at 500 ms, independent joint progress, slow movement in both directions, settled/uncommanded joints and small oscillations.
- Controller simulation: frozen fresh feedback queues STOP in 500–650 ms including command-lead startup; original event survives another STOP and controller restart with no motion replay.
- Existing control-guard, startup/recovery and synchronized/cycle tests passed.
- Native core builds treat warnings as errors. JavaScript syntax and shared-prompt regressions cover the incident UI.
- These fixtures do not validate physical contact sensing or guarantee delivery/braking time on hardware. Physical validation requires a controlled fixture and independent assessment.
